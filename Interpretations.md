# Activation Curve: Time-to-First-Meaningful-Action

### **What the query does**

Measures how quickly new signups become meaningful users and tracks **7-day activation** across weekly signup cohorts.

A meaningful action is the first `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, or `purchase`.

The output includes cohort size, 7-day activation count/rate, median activation time, and P90 activation time.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: `signup_cohort` creates weekly signup cohorts while applying the **2026-04-19 instrumentation cutoff**, and `first_action` identifies each customer's first meaningful action and calculates minutes from signup to activation. The final query aggregates these customer-level results by signup week.

### **Business interpretation**

7-day activation improved from **15.31%** for the April 20 cohort to **21.67%** for the May 18 cohort, before declining to **8.79%** by June 8. The June 8 result should be treated cautiously because the cohort may not have had a complete 7-day observation window.

Among customers who activated within 7 days, median activation time is generally around **2.8–3.2 days**, while P90 is around **5.5–6.3 days**, indicating that most activators take several days to perform their first meaningful action.

### **What I'd ask next**

- What caused activation to decline after the May 18 cohort?
- Which acquisition channels bring customers who activate faster?
- Which customer, product, or category segments have the strongest and weakest 7-day activation?
## Q2 — Checkout Funnel Leakage: Paid vs Organic

### **What the query does**

Measures checkout progression for paid and organic sessions from `begin_checkout` through `purchase`, and calculates the drop-off percentage at each step and overall.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: the first identifies the **maximum checkout step reached per session**, and the second counts sessions reaching each step by channel. Conditional aggregation is then used to calculate funnel drop-off.

### **Business interpretation**

Organic has slightly better checkout completion than paid (**86.15% vs 85.76%**), a **0.39 percentage-point difference**. The largest drop for both channels occurs between `begin_checkout` and `add_address`, while shipping and payment leakage are very similar.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Why is the address step the largest leakage point for both channels?
- Is the paid-vs-organic difference driven by product, customer, device, or campaign mix?
- Does the slightly higher paid-channel drop outweigh the revenue and customer value generated by paid acquisition?
