# Q1: Activation Curve: Time-to-First-Meaningful-Action

### **What the query does**

Measures how quickly new signups become meaningful users and tracks **7-day activation** across weekly signup cohorts.

A meaningful action is the first `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, or `purchase`.

The output includes cohort size, 7-day activation count/rate, median activation time, and P90 activation time.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: `signup_cohort` creates weekly signup cohorts while applying the **2026-04-19 instrumentation cutoff**, and `first_action` identifies each customer's first meaningful action and calculates minutes from signup to activation. The final query aggregates these customer-level results by signup week.

### **Business interpretation**

7-day activation improved from **15.31%** for the April 20 cohort to **21.67%** for the May 18 cohort, before declining to **8.79%** by June 8. The June 8 result should be treated cautiously because the cohort may not have had a complete 7-day observation window.

Among customers who activated within 7 days, median activation time is generally around **2.8–3.2 days**, while P90 is around **5.5–6.3 days**, indicating that most activators take several days to perform their first meaningful action.

### **What I'd ask next**

- What caused activation to decline after the May 18 cohort?
- Which acquisition channels bring customers who activate faster?
- Which customer, product, or category segments have the strongest and weakest 7-day activation?

## Q2 : Checkout Funnel Leakage: Paid vs Organic

### **What the query does**

Measures checkout progression for paid and organic sessions from `begin_checkout` through `purchase`, and calculates the drop-off percentage at each step and overall.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: the first identifies the **maximum checkout step reached per session**, and the second counts sessions reaching each step by channel. Conditional aggregation is then used to calculate funnel drop-off.

### **Business interpretation**

Organic has slightly better checkout completion than paid (**86.15% vs 85.76%**), a **0.39 percentage-point difference**. The largest drop for both channels occurs between `begin_checkout` and `add_address`, while shipping and payment leakage are very similar.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Why is the address step the largest leakage point for both channels?
- Is the paid-vs-organic difference driven by product, customer, device, or campaign mix?
- Does the slightly higher paid-channel drop outweigh the revenue and customer value generated by paid acquisition?

## Q3 - Weekly Cohort Retention Analysis

### **What the query does**

Tracks customer activity from **Week 0 to Week 4** based on their signup week.

The output shows **cohort size, active customers, retained customers, and retention rate**.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses a `LEFT JOIN` so that **customers without any session events are still included in the cohort size**.

- **Cohort size** → all customers who signed up from April 20 onward.
- **W0 active** → customers with activity in Week 0.
- **W1–W4 retained** → customers with meaningful activity (`product_view`, `add_to_cart`, `purchase`) in that week.

### **Business interpretation**

Retention varies across cohorts.

For example, the **April 20 cohort** has:

- **725 customers**
- **193 W0 active**
- **220 W1 retained (30%)**
- **207 W2 retained (29%)**
- **221 W3 retained (30%)**
- **265 W4 retained (37%)**

The latest cohorts have lower/zero retention because they **may not yet have completed the required observation period**.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Why does retention change across cohorts?
- Why did retention drop in the later cohorts?
- Which customer segments have the highest retention?
- Are the latest cohort results affected by incomplete observation windows?

# Q4: Product Conversion: Eyeballs → Add-to-Cart

### **What the query does**

Measures product views and **add-to-cart sessions**, then calculates each product's **ATC rate** and compares it with the **median ATC rate of its category**.

The output includes views, add-to-cart sessions, ATC rate, category-median comparison, and product rankings.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: `product_details` calculates product-level views, add-to-cart sessions, and ATC rate, while `category_median_atc` calculates the **median ATC rate for each category**. The final query joins both results to compare each product against its category benchmark.

### **Business interpretation**

Products with **high views but low ATC rate relative to their category median** are potential conversion problems. Customers are interested enough to view the product but are not progressing to the cart.

These flagged SKUs may indicate **price, image, or stock issues** and should be investigated by the merchandising team.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Is the low ATC rate caused by **price, image quality, or stock availability**?
- Would **discounts or promotions** improve ATC conversion?
- Which **top 10 high-view, low-relative-ATC products** should the merchandising PM investigate first?

# Q5 : Cart Abandonment: Cart Value → GMV Left on Table

### **What the query does**

Measures cart abandonment across different cart-value buckets and shows **how many ATC sessions are converted to purchase, how many are abandoned, and the GMV lost from abandoned carts**.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs: `session_with_amt` calculates the **total cart value per session**, while `cart_bucket` assigns each session to a cart-value bucket and identifies whether the session had a purchase event. The final query aggregates ATC sessions, purchases, abandonment rate, and abandoned GMV by bucket.

### **Business interpretation**

Abandonment is highest for **lower-value carts**, reaching **53% for carts below ₹500**, compared with only **12% for ₹15,000+ carts**. However, the largest GMV opportunity is in the **₹5,000+ buckets**, where abandoned carts account for about **₹15.7M** in potential GMV.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Should we prioritize **checkout reliability for ₹5,000+ carts**, since they contribute the most GMV left on the table?
- What **products, categories, and customer segments** are driving the high abandonment rate in the `<₹500` bucket?
- Can **free-shipping thresholds or promotions** improve conversion for low-value carts?

# Query S1 — Monthly MRR Movement Decomposition

### **What the query does**

Measures monthly MRR movement and identifies what drives the change: **new, expansion, contraction, churn, and reactivation**.

Output includes monthly MRR movements, **net new MRR**, and **ending MRR**.

### **Pattern choice**

Uses two CTEs:

- `classified` → assigns each event to an MRR bucket.
- `monthly_mrr` → aggregates MRR movement by month.

`ending_mrr` is calculated cumulatively from monthly `net_new_mrr`.

The analysis excludes the **234 future-dated legacy rows** using the `2026-06-15` cutoff.

### **Business interpretation**

- MRR increased from **₹169.92 in Aug 2025** to **₹164.32K in Jun 2026**.
- **March 2026** had the largest churn spike: **₹13.82K**, ~**2.8× February**.
- Despite the churn spike, March still generated **₹12.05K net new MRR**.
- **May 2026** recorded the highest new MRR at **₹24.17K**.
- Overall MRR growth was driven mainly by **new and expansion MRR** outweighing contraction and churn.

### **What I'd ask next**

- Why did churn MRR spike in March?
- Which signup cohort contributed most to the spike?
- Which plans or acquisition channels had the highest churn?
- Did churned customers show weaker engagement before cancellation?
